# Bipolar Disorders

Status: curriculum draft v0.1 (2026-11-22); educational, not diagnostic or individualized treatment advice. Review by a qualified subject-matter expert before public release.

## Learning objectives
- Differentiate mania and hypomania
- contrast bipolar I and II
- explain why ordinary mood swings are not diagnostic.

## Lesson 1 — Core concepts
Bipolar disorders involve episodes of altered mood, energy and activity. Mania can include reduced need for sleep, elevated or irritable mood, increased activity, impulsivity and sometimes psychosis. Hypomania is less severe than mania; bipolar I involves at least one manic episode, while bipolar II involves hypomania and major depression without a history of mania. Ordinary mood fluctuations do not establish bipolar disorder. Psychotherapy, routines and sleep regularity may support care, but bipolar disorder commonly requires professional long-term treatment; lifestyle strategies should not be presented as replacements for indicated clinical care.

## Lesson 2 — Critical thinking
Compare symptoms, duration, impairment and context. Avoid diagnosing yourself or another person from an article. Distinguish established findings from emerging hypotheses and ask whether claims are supported by clinical guidelines or peer-reviewed evidence.
