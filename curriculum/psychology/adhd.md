# Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (formerly ADD)

Status: curriculum draft v0.1 (2026-11-22); educational, not diagnostic or individualized treatment advice. Review by a qualified subject-matter expert before public release.

## Learning objectives
- Identify the three ADHD presentations
- distinguish occasional distraction from clinically significant impairment
- explain why differential assessment matters.

## Lesson 1 — Core concepts
ADD is an older term, generally corresponding to ADHD with predominantly inattentive presentation. ADHD is a neurodevelopmental condition involving persistent inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity. Presentations are inattentive, hyperactive-impulsive and combined. Symptoms begin before age 12, persist for at least six months, affect more than one setting and cause functional impairment. Sleep problems, stress and other conditions can resemble ADHD. Supports may include organizational strategies, environmental adjustments, behavioral interventions and professional care; an educational quiz cannot diagnose ADHD.

## Lesson 2 — Critical thinking
Compare symptoms, duration, impairment and context. Avoid diagnosing yourself or another person from an article. Distinguish established findings from emerging hypotheses and ask whether claims are supported by clinical guidelines or peer-reviewed evidence.
