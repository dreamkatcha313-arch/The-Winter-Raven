Foundations of Neuroscience

Introductory study path · 6 short readings · Review pending

Lesson 1 — Neurons and glia

Neurons transmit information through electrical and chemical signaling. Glia support, protect and regulate neural environments; they are not merely structural filler.

Reflection: Explain this concept in your own words and identify one limitation or misconception.

Lesson 2 — Synapses

An action potential can trigger neurotransmitter release at a chemical synapse. Receptors determine how a receiving cell responds.

Reflection: Explain this concept in your own words and identify one limitation or misconception.

Lesson 3 — Brain networks

Attention, memory and emotion depend on interacting networks, not single isolated brain centers.

Reflection: Explain this concept in your own words and identify one limitation or misconception.

Lesson 4 — Plasticity

Experience can change synaptic strength and network activity. Plasticity does not guarantee recovery or mastery.

Reflection: Explain this concept in your own words and identify one limitation or misconception.

Lesson 5 — Learning and memory

Retrieval practice and spaced review can improve retention; sleep supports memory processes.

Reflection: Explain this concept in your own words and identify one limitation or misconception.

Lesson 6 — Research literacy

Imaging correlations do not establish causation. Consider comparison groups, sample size and replication.

Childhood ADHD

ADHD is a neurodevelopmental condition; inattention and impulsivity are not evidence of poor parenting.

Reflection: Explain this concept in your own words and identify one limitation or misconception.

Clinicians consider developmental history, impairment across settings and alternative explanations such as sleep or learning problems.

Use short instructions, predictable routines and specific praise for achievable behaviors. Adapt expectations to the child's developmental needs.

Lesson 4 — School support

Collaborate with teachers on visual schedules, task breakdown and appropriate accommodations.

Traumatic exposure can lead to many different responses; PTSD is not inevitable.

PTSD includes intrusion, avoidance, negative changes in cognition and mood, and changes in arousal and reactivity.

Bipolar disorders

Bipolar disorders involve distinct episodes affecting mood, energy and activity, not simply everyday mood changes.

Reflection: Explain this concept in your own words and identify one limitation or misconception.

Mania may involve increased energy, reduced need for sleep and impaired judgment; severe episodes can require urgent care.

Reflection: Explain this concept in your own words and identify one limitation or misconception.

Lesson 3 — Hypomania

Hypomania is less severe than mania and does not cause the marked impairment characteristic of mania.

Reflection: Explain this concept in your own words and identify one limitation or misconception.

Lesson 4 — Bipolar I and II

Bipolar I requires a manic episode; bipolar II involves hypomania and major depression without a history of mania.

Reflection: Explain this concept in your own words and identify one limitation or misconception.

Lesson 5 — Support

Sleep regularity, routines and professional follow-up may support management but do not replace indicated treatment.

Reflection: Explain this concept in your own words and identify one limitation or misconception.

Lesson 6 — Assessment

A clinician considers episode history, other conditions and substances. Do not diagnose from a short mood questionnaire.
